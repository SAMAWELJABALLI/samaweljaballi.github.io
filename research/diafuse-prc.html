      DiaFuse-PRC · 2026

      DiaFuse-PRC: An Explainable Analysis of Dialect-Aware Multi-Encoder Fusion with Prototype-Guided Contrastive Learning for Cross-Script Sentiment Transfer

      SubmittedManuscript submitted to an international journal; under review.

      Arabizi sentiment classifiers are often trained on Arabic-script text and its synthetic Latin-script transliterations, then tested on human Arabizi from other corpora, where script, spelling, corpus and dialect all change. The study evaluates DiaFuse-PRC, a fusion of three frozen encoders with class–variety prototypes, along a controlled seven-stage test chain. Tunisian-trained models lose more on Moroccan Arabizi along a dialect-associated contrast than along the script.

      Overview

      Problem

      Sentiment models trained on Arabic-script Tunisian data and on synthetic transliterations of it must hold up on human-written Arabizi and on other Maghrebi varieties.

      Approach

      DiaFuse-PRC fuses three frozen encoders (XLM-R, mE5, LaBSE) through an input-dependent gate, with hierarchical class–variety prototypes, rebalanced contrastive learning, transliteration consistency and variety-wise calibration.

      Evaluation protocol

      Training data
      Arabic-script Tunisian sentences (TSAC) and synthetic Latin-script transliterations of them only.
      Test chain
      Seven stages adding one shift each: S0 TSAC Arabic script; S1 seen transliteration generator; S2 unseen generator; S3 human TSAC-Latin; S4 TUNIZI; S5 NArabizi (Algerian); S6 MYC (Moroccan).
      Controls
      Script-controlled counterparts of the external test sets: S5AR, S6AR and S6syn.
      Encoders
      XLM-R, mE5 and LaBSE, frozen; a fully fine-tuned XLM-R-large baseline is included among the twelve Tunisian-trained systems.
      Runs
      Three seeds with default hyper-parameters; the archived run is reduced and provisional.
      Pre-registration
      The follow-up DiaFuse-PRC++ is pre-registered in the repository and not yet executed.

      Main results

      • On Moroccan Arabizi, the loss along the dialect-associated contrast exceeds the loss along the script: 27.5 against 15.1 macro-F1 points for DiaFuse-PRC; the ordering is significant for all twelve Tunisian-trained systems.
      • DiaFuse-PRC averages 66.1 macro-F1 over the four human stages: equal to static concatenation, below the best single encoder (67.1) and a fully fine-tuned baseline (69.7).
      • Post-hoc analyses: the gate routed every input to one encoder; the encoders' errors leave 12.1 points of oracle headroom beyond a seed-noise control, but combining them without target labels gains only 0.6 points over the best encoder.
      • A linear probe on the best frozen encoder reaches 69.8 macro-F1 on Moroccan labels, whereas the same probe trained on Tunisian data reaches 50.1.

      Source: abstract of the manuscript (three-seed run with default hyper-parameters).
